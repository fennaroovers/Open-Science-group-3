**Gearbox Assembly Guide**

This guide explains how to assemble, adjust, install, and test the 3D-printed gearbox after printing all the required parts at Lilly's Protolab.
The gearbox consists mainly of 3D-printed structural parts and gears, together with a DC motor and standard hardware available in the laboratory. Since small dimensional differences are normal with 3D-printed components, some adjustment may be required during assembly, especially when positioning the gears and selecting washers.
Use the reference pictures throughout the assembly process. The position, orientation, and order of the gears are particularly important.

**Step 1 — Collect All the 3D-Printed Parts**

After printing the gearbox parts at Lilly's Protolab, gather all the printed components and place them together on a clean working surface, as shown in the first reference picture.
Before starting the assembly, inspect every part and remove any remaining support material, loose filament, or printing imperfections that could interfere with the movement of the gears.
Make sure you have all the main printed components, including:
- the two gearbox side plates,
- the compound gears,
- the small motor pinion gear,
- the output gear/shaft components,
- and any other printed structural parts required for the assembly.

**Step 2 — Collect the Additional Hardware**

The 3D-printed parts are not enough to complete the gearbox. You will also need several non-printed components.
The required hardware can be found in the drawers and storage areas of the laboratory. Collect the necessary components before continuing.
These include:
- screws,
- nuts,
- washers,
- spacers if required,
- the DC motor,
The motor is an essential part of the system, so make sure you have the appropriate motor before completing the gearbox.
At this stage, it is useful to keep several different washers available. You may need to experiment with their thickness and material later to obtain the correct gear spacing.

**Step 3 — Prepare the First Side Plate**

- Take one of the two orange 3D-printed side plates and place it flat on the working surface.
- Insert the screws/shafts that will support the gears and connect the two sides of the gearbox. Use the reference pictures carefully to determine the correct holes and orientation.
- Check that each shaft is reasonably straight and perpendicular to the side plate.
- This is important because poor shaft alignment can cause the gears to sit at an angle, resulting in unnecessary friction or even preventing the gearbox from rotating.
- Do not fully tighten everything yet. Keeping the assembly slightly adjustable will make it easier to correct the gear alignment later.

**Step 4 — Install the First Compound Gear**

- Place the first compound gear onto its corresponding shaft, following the orientation shown in the reference pictures.
- Make sure that the gear can rotate freely around its shaft.
- Do not force a gear onto a shaft. If it does not rotate properly, inspect the hole and shaft for excess printed material or incorrect alignment before continuing.
- At this point, you can also begin experimenting with washers if the gear is rubbing directly against the side plate.

**Step 5 — Install the Remaining Gears in the Correct Order**

- Continue installing the remaining gears one by one. This is one of the most important stages of the entire assembly.
- Pay close attention to the reference pictures to determine which gear is installed first, second, third, and so on. The large and small sections of the compound gears must be positioned correctly so that each gear meshes with the next stage.
- The purpose of this arrangement is to transfer the rotational motion through the complete gear train. Therefore, an incorrectly positioned or reversed gear can prevent the entire gearbox from operating.
- After installing each gear, rotate the mechanism manually.
- Do not wait until the gearbox is completely assembled before checking whether the gears move. Testing after every new gear makes it much easier to identify where a problem was introduced.

**Step 6 — Adjust the Gear Spacing with Washers**

- Correct spacing between the gears is extremely important.
- Because the gearbox components are 3D printed, small dimensional variations between prints are normal. For this reason, the ideal washer arrangement may need to be determined experimentally rather than following one fixed configuration.
- Washers can be placed between gears or between a gear and the gearbox side plate to adjust the axial position of each component.
- You can experiment with different washers available in the laboratory, including:
    - plastic washers, which can provide spacing with relatively low friction,
    - metal washers, which can provide more rigid and precise spacing.
- Try different configurations and rotate the gears manually after each adjustment.
- The objective is to achieve a configuration where the gears:
    - mesh correctly,
    - rotate freely,
    - do not rub excessively against each other or the side plates,
    - and do not have excessive lateral movement.
- Do not simply add as many washers as possible. Too much spacing can be just as problematic as too little spacing because the gears may no longer align correctly.

**Step 7 — Check the Complete Gear Train**

Once the main gears have been installed, manually rotate the gear train before closing the gearbox.
When you rotate one gear, the movement should be transferred through the neighboring gears.
Check carefully that:
- all gears rotate,
- neighboring gear teeth mesh correctly,
- the gears are correctly aligned,
- no gear is strongly rubbing against the side plate,
- the gears do not wobble excessively,
- and the mechanism does not become stuck at any point during a complete rotation.
Some resistance can be expected from a 3D-printed gear system, but the mechanism should not require significant force to rotate.
If something becomes stuck, do not force it. Check the gear orientation, washers, spacing, and shaft alignment instead.

**Step 8 — Do Not Forget the Small Motor Pinion Gear**

- Make sure that the small pinion gear is included in the assembly.
- Although it is one of the smallest components in the gearbox, it has a critical function: it provides the mechanical connection between the DC motor and the rest of the gear train.
- The small pinion is driven directly by the motor and transfers its rotation to the first larger gear.
- Carefully position the small gear so that it correctly engages with the first driven gear of the gearbox.
**IMPORTANT: If this small gear is missing or incorrectly positioned, the motor may rotate normally, but its rotation will not be transferred to the rest of the gearbox.**
- Check the contact between the pinion and the first larger gear. The teeth should mesh reliably without being pressed excessively against each other.
- If they are too close, friction can increase significantly. If they are too far apart, the teeth may slip or fail to engage.

**Step 9 — Position the DC Motor**

- Install the DC motor in its correct position at the end of the gearbox.
- Make sure that the motor is properly seated and that its shaft is correctly aligned with the small pinion gear and the first stage of the transmission.
- Before closing the gearbox, manually rotate the motor shaft, when possible, and observe the gears.
- The motion should follow the complete transmission path:
DC Motor → Small Pinion Gear → Compound Gear Stages → Output Shaft
- If the first gear does not move correctly, inspect the position of the motor pinion before continuing.

**Step 10 — Perform a Final Test Before Closing the Gearbox**

- Before attaching the second side plate, perform another manual test of the complete mechanism.
- Slowly rotate the gears and observe every stage of the transmission.
- This is the best time to make adjustments because all the gears are still accessible.
- If necessary:
    - change the position of a washer,
    - replace a metal washer with a plastic one or vice versa,
    - adjust the position of a gear,
    - check the shaft alignment,
    - or slightly reposition the motor/pinion.

**Step 11 — Attach the Second Side Plate**
  
- Once you are satisfied with the gear alignment, carefully position the second 3D-printed side plate over the assembly.
- Guide the screws, shafts, spacers, and output shaft through their corresponding holes.
- Do not force the side plate into position. If it does not fit easily, check whether one of the shafts or gears is misaligned.
- Install the nuts and begin tightening the screws gradually.
- Do not fully tighten one screw while the others are loose. Instead, tighten them progressively and evenly. This helps keep the two side plates parallel and prevents unnecessary pressure on the gear train.

**Step 12 — Test the Gearbox Again After Tightening**

- After the two side plates have been secured, manually rotate the gearbox again.
- This test is important because the mechanism may rotate correctly while open but become difficult to rotate after the screws are tightened.
- If this happens, the problem is likely related to:
    - excessive pressure between the side plates,
    - incorrect washer thickness,
    - a gear rubbing against the housing,
    - or a misaligned shaft.
- Slightly loosen the assembly and make the necessary adjustments.
- The gearbox should be secure, but the gears must still be able to rotate smoothly.

**Step 13 — Install the Gearbox in the Black Holder**
  
- Once the gearbox operates correctly, place the complete assembly inside the black 3D-printed holder/housing, as shown in the reference pictures.
- Position it carefully so that the motor, output shaft, and any components required for testing remain accessible.
- Make sure that the black holder does not touch or obstruct any rotating part.
- The holder can then be secured to the test setup. In the setup shown in the reference pictures, a clamp is used to keep the assembly firmly in place.
- Safety Note: Secure the gearbox before powering the motor. Do not rely on holding the complete assembly in your hand during powered testing.

**Step 14 — Connect the DC Motor**

- With the gearbox securely mounted, connect the electrical leads to the two terminals of the DC motor.
- Connect the motor only to a suitable DC power supply within the motor's specified voltage and current limits.
- Before applying power, check that:
    - the gearbox is securely mounted,
    - the small motor pinion is installed,
    - the gears are correctly aligned,
    - the output shaft can rotate freely,
    - the electrical connections are secure,
    - the two motor connections are not shorted together,
    - and no hands, tools, wires, or loose objects are close to the moving gears.
- If possible, begin testing at a low motor speed/voltage within the motor's permitted operating range.

**Step 15 — Power the Motor and Observe the Gear Train**
  
- Apply power to the motor and observe the mechanism.
- The motor should rotate the small pinion gear. The pinion should then drive the first larger gear, transferring the rotation through each compound gear until it reaches the output shaft.
- The sequence should therefore be:
Motor → Pinion → Gear Stage 1 → Gear Stage 2 → Gear Stage 3 → Output
- Watch and listen to the gearbox during the first test.
- If the motor rotates but the rest of the gearbox does not, disconnect the power and check the small pinion gear first.
- If the gears move but repeatedly jam, skip teeth, vibrate excessively, or require the motor to struggle, disconnect the power rather than allowing the motor to continue forcing the mechanism.
- Return to the mechanical assembly and check the washers, gear alignment, shaft positioning, and screw tension.

**Step 16 — Check the Direction of Rotation**

- The direction of a brushed DC motor can normally be reversed by reversing the polarity applied to its two terminals.
- If the output shaft rotates in the opposite direction from the one required for your application, disconnect the power first, reverse the motor connections, and test again.
- There is no need to rearrange the gear train simply to reverse the output direction.

**Step 17 — Final Adjustment and Testing**

- Run the gearbox for a short test while observing the complete mechanism.
- The final assembly should:
    - transfer rotation continuously from the motor to the output shaft,
    - rotate without gears becoming stuck,
    - maintain proper contact between neighboring gears,
    - avoid excessive friction between gears and side plates,
    - remain mechanically stable inside the black holder,
    - and operate without excessive vibration or gear skipping.
- If necessary, disconnect the power and repeat the washer-spacing adjustments until the best mechanical performance is achieved.
- Remember that 3D-printed components will not always have exactly the same tolerances, so small adjustments are expected.

**Important Assembly Notes**
  
The most important part of this project is not simply fitting all the components together. A gearbox can look correctly assembled while still performing poorly if the gears are misaligned or compressed against each other.
For this reason, test the gears manually throughout the entire assembly process. After adding a new gear, check the movement. After changing a washer, check the movement again. After tightening the side plates, check it again before connecting electrical power.
The washers should be treated as an adjustment mechanism. Try plastic or metal washers and different thicknesses until you obtain suitable spacing between the gears.
Finally, do not forget the small motor pinion gear. It is easy to overlook because of its size, but it is essential: without it, there is no mechanical connection between the motor and the rest of the gearbox.
All standard hardware required for the assembly, such as screws, nuts, washers, and other small components, can be found in the laboratory drawers.
